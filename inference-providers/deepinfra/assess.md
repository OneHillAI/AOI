## Data governance - retention, ZDR & training

Zero Data Retention is contractual and controlling in the Terms of Service (last modified
2026-10-15). Sec 7(b): *"Provider will not retain, store, or log any Customer Data submitted to
or generated by the Services beyond the period strictly necessary to process and return the
applicable request... ('Zero Data Retention')"*, and Sec 5(a) says this commitment "controls over
the Privacy Policy and any other Provider policy, webpage, or documentation". Training is barred
except as necessary to provide the Services (for example a fine-tune you ask for). The exceptions
are bounded: support data kept at your written request and deleted within 30 days of resolution,
non-content metadata, legal and abuse records, and data kept for a feature you elect (batch
requests, asynchronous media outputs, third-party integrations or paid cache retention) for the
documented period.

ZDR is scoped: it "applies to models hosted by Provider". The Terms state in the binding text that
data you send to a third-party (partner) model is governed by that partner's terms. In the live
listing, 94 of 232 public entries are flagged partner, including Anthropic Claude and Google
Gemini, and the trust-centre FAQ says partner models "run on the
partner's infrastructure, and the partner's data policy applies". Segregate open-model and partner-model traffic when governance
matters. Nothing independent attests the ZDR itself.

## Data & IP ownership

For DeepInfra-hosted models, Sec 7(b) leaves all IP in Customer Data with you, gives the provider
only a licence to host and process it to provide the Services, and bars sale and training. Sec 17
is a mutual confidentiality duty with three-year survival. Two limits apply: the definition is
general and does not name Customer Data as Confidential Information, and Sec 17(f) lets the
provider use your name and logo in marketing. Ownership is not uniform across the catalogue:
partner models carry the partner's terms. Sub-processors are disclosed (AWS, Google Cloud
Platform, Stripe, all United States), but Sec 6(a) lets DeepInfra engage new ones "without
Customer's consent". The trust-centre FAQ says the DPA gives 7 days notice, which is unverified
because the DPA PDF is blocked.

## Compliance & attestations

Terms Sec 5(a) incorporates a data processing agreement "by reference" that applies to personal
data in Customer Data, with no separate signature. The trust centre lists it as
"2026-10-05 - Deep Infra - DPA" and its FAQ says it "includes the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Modules 1 to 3), the UK IDTA
and Swiss terms", and that DeepInfra is not certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. The
DPA PDF returns HTTP 403 from CloudFront, so the SCC content is a publisher statement.

The trust centre shows SOC 2 and ISO 27001 as "Compliant", GDPR as "In
progress" and HIPAA as "Compliant". It lists a SOC 2 Type 1 report with a bridge letter, an ISO 27001 certificate, an
ISMS manual, a pen-test report and a BAA, all unverified. A BAA needs an enterprise agreement and
covers hosted models only. Read compliance as: DPA in place, attestations claimed and unverified.

## Data residency & jurisdiction

The trust-centre FAQ says "All DeepInfra inference runs in data
centers in the United States", for both the serverless API and dedicated deployments, with "no EU
or other non-US region" and no choice of US region. The Privacy Policy says data is processed
"specifically in the United States". The SCCs in the DPA give a transfer mechanism, not residency. As a US-headquartered
company, DeepInfra carries standing US CLOUD Act exposure. For workloads with EU residency
requirements this is a hard constraint.

<!-- item: security -->
## Security controls

The trust centre states SOC 2 and ISO 27001 as "Compliant", shows control families (MFA, network
and endpoint controls, vulnerability remediation) and lists an ISMS manual and a pen-test report.
The docs and FAQ say inference input is held in memory only, batch data is encrypted on disk then
deleted, and the prompt cache is in memory per account and model. The assessment is held to
partial because no certificate, SOC 2 report or pen-test report is verified, SOC 2 is Type 1, and
tenant isolation on shared endpoints is not described in the sources.

## Pricing & cost model

First-party list prices (USD per million tokens, input / output): Llama 3.1 8B Turbo 0.02 / 0.04,
Llama 3.3 70B Turbo 0.10 / 0.32, gpt-oss-120B 0.037 / 0.17, DeepSeek V4 Flash 0.09 / 0.18, Kimi
K3.1 Pro 2.00 / 10.00. Service tiers add Priority (1.5x) and Flex (0.8x). Dedicated GPUs cost
A100 $1.49, H100 $2.90, H200 $3.49, B200 $4.89 and B300 $5.89 per GPU-hour, 59% to 75% above the
July 2026 figures. A card or prepayment is required and a spending limit can be set. Serverless
prices are very low, but "among the cheapest" is not supported for dedicated capacity, so cost
scores 4.

## Reliability posture

A public status page (status.deepinfra.com) shows measured 90-day uptime for the API and website
of 100% and per-model figures of 99.98% to 100%, a maintenance page and scheduled deprecations
with replacements. There is no uptime SLA or credits; Terms Sec 4(b) offers only a 24-hour repair
or replacement of a failed provided server. The page has no incident-history view, so past
incident communication cannot be reviewed. DeepInfra suits cost-led work, not workloads that need
a contractual availability commitment.
